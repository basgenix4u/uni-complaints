# The Nigerian university sector, and where Resolve fits

Desk research, September 2026. Sources are cited so the reasoning can be
checked rather than taken on trust. Nothing here replaces talking to a
real registrar, which remains the most valuable thing still undone.

---

## 1. How big the market actually is

The National Universities Commission recognised **309 universities** as
of early 2026, after approving 33 new ones during 2025.

| Ownership | Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Private | 168 | The largest single category, and growing fastest |
| Federal | 74 | Includes the specialised technology and agriculture universities |
| State | 67 | Governed separately from the federal ones, which matters below |

Polytechnics and colleges of education are **not** in that figure. They
fall under the NBTE and NCCE respectively, are regulated separately, and
represent a further several hundred institutions.

Two things follow for us.

**Private universities are the better first customer.** They are the
largest group, they compete on student experience, they make decisions
without a state ministry in the loop, and they have a commercial reason
to be seen handling complaints well. A federal university's procurement
process would outlast our patience.

**The sector is not short of institutions.** It is short of institutions
that have solved this problem. A product that works at one university is
immediately relevant to three hundred others.

## 2. What students actually complain about

The reported pattern maps closely onto the categories already built,
which is reassuring but was partly luck.

**Results and academic administration.** In 2025, 44 final-year Pharmacy
students at Olabisi Onabanjo University risked losing a year because
result approvals were delayed past their induction date. Parents and
alumni protested; the Dean asked for patience. This is precisely the
`result_issues` and `examination` path, and precisely the failure the
escalation ladder exists for.

**Money.** Fees, receipts that do not reflect, scholarship
administration. The `fees_payment` route to Bursary is the highest
volume category in most complaint systems.

**Sexual harassment, and the institutional response to it.** This is
the most serious finding, and it is worth stating plainly.

The research is consistent and damning:

- Reporting mechanisms "lack legitimacy among stakeholders", with
  students citing **mistrust, lack of confidentiality, and opaque
  investigative procedures** as reasons for not engaging.
- Institutions "often prioritise institutional reputation over justice".
  Complaints are "delayed, quietly settled, or ignored entirely".
- Allegations against influential staff are "sometimes handled
  informally or not pursued at all".
- In one 2025 study, **22.9% of students did not know the complaint
  process existed at all** and a further 27.6% were unsure of it.
- A widely reported pattern: alleged perpetrators were **not**
  first-time offenders. Students had spoken "in hushed tones" and used
  "informal channels", and nothing happened until public attention
  forced it.

A commentator writing in 2025 called for exactly what this product is:
*"an independent, anonymously accessible, and professionally managed
reporting system that operates outside the influence of university
management."*

There is still **no national law** specifically covering sexual
harassment in Nigerian higher education; a bill has been pending since
before 2023.

**Strikes.** ASUU industrial action is a constant. Three Lagos state
universities were shut indefinitely in September 2026. This is not
something a complaints system solves, but it shapes the environment: a
student body that has learned institutions do not respond is a student
body that needs convincing the ticket goes somewhere.

## 3. What this means for the product

Three conclusions, and one uncomfortable one.

**The confidential queue is the most important feature, not a
refinement.** It was built in stage 3 and I described it then as
safeguarding. The research says it is the entire value proposition for
the highest-stakes complaint category. A harassment report that the
accused lecturer's own department cannot read, with an audit trail
showing who opened it, addresses the specific documented failure:
complaints quietly buried by people close to the accused.

**Anonymous reporting matters more than I credited.** You insisted on it
early. The literature explains why: the fear is not embarrassment, it is
"academic suicide" — manipulated grades, blocked research, delayed
degrees. Anonymity is not a nicety for these users.

**The ignored-complaints report is the differentiator.** Every
institution can claim it has a complaints process. Few can show what
happened to complaints nobody answered. That report, plus the escalation
ladder, is the part a university cannot fake.

**The uncomfortable one.** If institutions bury complaints to protect
their reputation, then a system that makes burial visible is not
obviously something they want to buy. That tension is real and I do not
think it is fatal — a university that adopts this is signalling
something, and the ones that will adopt it first are the ones already
trying to do better. But it should be named rather than glossed over.

## 4. Where the product is weakest against this evidence

Honest gaps, from comparing what is built against what the research
describes.

**No appeals path.** Every serious grievance framework — the UGC model
in India, the OIA in the UK, the Australian state ombudsmen — has a
second tier: a student dissatisfied with the outcome escalates to
someone independent. We escalate on *silence* but not on *disagreement*.
A student told "we investigated and found nothing" has nowhere to go.
This is stage 8 in the plan and it is more important than its position
suggests.

**No published outcomes.** The Australian ombudsman research is
emphatic: institutions should "provide and publish progress reports and
final outcome reports of complaints, including the types and outcomes".
We produce this data and expose it only to staff. An anonymised public
summary would be a strong trust signal, and is not built.

**Nothing for the accused.** The same research warns about false or
frivolous complaints and recommends procedural fairness both ways.
Someone named in a complaint currently has no defined right to respond
before an outcome is recorded.

**Polytechnics and colleges are excluded by vocabulary.** You asked me
to set this aside, and that was right at the time. But NBTE and NCCE
institutions are a large adjacent market and the only real barrier is
that we say Faculty and Vice-Chancellor where they say School and
Rector.

## 5. A realistic go-to-market

Not a plan, a sequence. Each step is cheap and tells you whether to take
the next one.

1. **One private university, one faculty, two weeks.** Private because
   the decision is fast. One faculty because the routing table is a
   guess until a real registrar corrects it.
2. **Let the SUG carry it.** Student unions have the distribution and
   the motive. An institution that adopts this via its SUG gets
   credibility a management rollout cannot buy.
3. **Publish the anonymised numbers, with the institution's consent.**
   "847 complaints, 71% resolved within the deadline" is a recruiting
   asset for them and a case study for us.
4. **Then approach the ones nearby.** Nigerian universities watch each
   other. The second sale is much easier than the first.

The product is free, so none of this is a revenue plan. It is a
credibility plan, and credibility is the scarce thing here.
